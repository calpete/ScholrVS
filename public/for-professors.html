The problem

General-purpose chatbots don't know what you taught.

When students reach for an off-the-shelf AI, it answers from the whole internet — different notation, different conventions, sometimes flatly contradicting your lecture. You lose the thread of what your class actually learned, and students can't tell what's reliable. Scholr is built to stay inside the four walls of your course.

Common questions

What faculty ask first.

Does it just make things up?

Scholr is designed to answer from the materials you upload rather than from open web text. When a question falls outside your uploaded content, it's built to say so instead of guessing — though like any AI tool, you should review it for your subject before relying on it broadly.

Will it do students' work for them?

The goal is understanding, not answer keys. You can pilot it as a study aid alongside your existing assignments and see how your students use it before deciding how it fits your course.

What do I have to upload?

Whatever you'd normally hand a student: lecture slides, notes, problem sets, readings you have the rights to share with your class. More material means more useful answers.

How do I try it?

We're running pilots with individual professors and departments. Request a pilot and we'll set up your first course together.
