About

AI tutoring that stays inside the course.

Scholr started from a simple frustration: students lean on AI that has no idea what their class actually covered — and professors have no idea what students are struggling with until grades come in.

What we believe

The course is the source of truth.

A tutor is only useful if it speaks the language of the class it's helping with. So Scholr answers from the materials a professor uploads — their notation, their methods, their framing — rather than a generic version scraped from the web. The same instinct runs through the product: keep each course private, keep students on the same page as their instructor, and give that instructor a clear view of where understanding is breaking down.

What we're building

Two sides of the same loop.

/ students

Help that matches the class

Answers grounded in the course's own materials, available whenever a student gets stuck.

/ instructors

A read on confusion

Analytics that show the topics students keep returning to, so teaching can respond before the exam does.

Who's behind it

A student-built tool.

Scholr is built by a student who got tired of watching classmates trust AI that didn't know the syllabus. We're working with professors and departments to pilot it in real courses.
