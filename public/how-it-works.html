How it works

Course in. Grounded answers out.

Scholr turns the materials a professor already has into a tutor that stays inside the course — and quietly reports back where students are struggling.

The flow

Four steps, start to finish.

The professor uploads the course

Slides, lecture notes, problem sets, readings — whatever defines the class. This becomes the source Scholr is allowed to answer from.

Students join and ask

Students enter the course with a join code and ask questions in plain language, any time of day.

Answers stay grounded in the material

Scholr draws its responses from the uploaded content, matching the course's notation and framing instead of the open web's.

The professor sees the confusion

A dashboard surfaces the topics students keep returning to, so instructors know what to reteach before it shows up on an exam.

Questions

Good to know.

What does Scholr base its answers on?

The course materials a professor uploads — not the open internet — so explanations line up with what the class is actually being taught.

Do students need an account or a code?

Students join a specific course, usually with a short join code their professor shares. Each course is private to the people in it.

What do professors see?

A dashboard of the questions and topics students return to most — a read on where the class is confused, before an exam makes it obvious.
